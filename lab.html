01 — Negative harmony

Flip any chord through the mirror.

In a 2017 interview Jacob explained negative harmony, and a lot of people went back to their theory books. The idea comes from Ernst Levy: draw an axis through the circle of fifths, between the tonic and the dominant, then reflect every note across it.

Major turns minor. G7 turns into Fm6. The cadence still lands, but it feels like it happened underwater.

Progression · key of C

02 — Microtonal drift

How a choir sings its way into G half-sharp.

Jacob's arrangement of In the Bleak Midwinter famously visits G half-sharp, a key that sits in the crack between two piano keys. One way a choir can get somewhere like that: pure tuning.

Each chord here is tuned perfectly, with none of the piano's compromises, and every note two chords share is held dead still. Loop G – D – Am – Em and the whole choir rises about 21.5 cents per lap. That's the syntonic comma. Two laps and a bit puts you a quarter-tone up.

03 — Audience choir

Conduct a whole room.

At almost every show Jacob splits the crowd into sections, hands each one a note and stacks thousands of strangers into harmony. Your turn.

Press on a section to hand it a note. Higher on the stage means a higher note. Let go and they keep holding it. Then move to the next section.

05 — Split Screen

Fill the grid. Every square is you.

Before the arenas, Jacob’s videos were split screens: a grid of Jacobs, every part sung or played by him. His cover of Stevie Wonder’s Don’t You Worry ’Bout a Thing is the one most people remember.

Your turn to be the whole band. Pick a square, tap the pads and the loop plays your part back to you. Every pad fits the chord that’s playing, so there are no wrong notes, only bolder ones.

06 — Reharm reels

Same tune. Braver chords.

Keep a melody everyone knows and change the chords underneath it. It sits at the heart of how Jacob arranges: his first two Grammys, in 2017, were both for arrangements, You and I and Flintstones, from In My Room.

Pick a tune, turn up the spice and spin. Every chord the reels can land on fits the melody. Lock the ones you love, spin the rest, and flip back to Before to hear how far you’ve come.

07 — Downbeat

Nothing moves until you do.

Last time you handed out notes. This time you keep the time. Tap the stage once per beat and a small choir and band play Ode to Joy at exactly your speed. Stop tapping and they hold the chord and wait for you.

Tap higher to play louder, and tap in a section’s lane to bring it in. Djesse Vol. 1 was recorded with a full orchestra, the Metropole Orkest. This one is pocket-sized, and it follows you.
